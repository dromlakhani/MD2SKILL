# FibroScan (VCTE) Report Interpretation in MASLD

A FibroScan gives two numbers: **LSM (liver stiffness, kPa)** — a surrogate for fibrosis — and **CAP (controlled attenuation parameter, dB/m)** — a surrogate for steatosis. **LSM drives prognosis and decisions. CAP does not.**

---

## Step 1 — Check the scan is interpretable

Before reading the numbers, confirm:

- **The patient has MASLD context** — this skill uses MASLD cut-offs. Viral, alcohol-related, or cholestatic disease use different thresholds
- **Body habitus:** the guideline notes that adults with **class 2 obesity often cannot be reliably examined** with ultrasound elastography, and that **obesity and underweight can falsely raise LSM**. Check which probe was used (M vs XL) and whether the operator flagged a difficult acquisition
- **Reproducibility:** VCTE is variable — coefficient of variation **up to 60% in cirrhosis**, and values **> 7.9 kPa are not reproduced in about one-third** on repeat. Don't act on one borderline value alone

**Device-quality checks (not specified in the EASL guideline — standard manufacturer / practice criteria, verify against your local protocol):**

- ≥ 10 valid measurements
- IQR/median ratio ≤ 30% (report usually shows this)
- Patient fasted before the scan
- No acute hepatitis flare, liver congestion (e.g. heart failure), or cholestasis at the time — each can falsely raise LSM

If the scan fails these checks, **repeat it or use another test** (ELF, MRE, 2D-SWE) rather than interpreting it.

---

## Step 2 — Interpret liver stiffness (LSM, kPa)

| LSM (VCTE) | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **< 8 kPa** | **Advanced fibrosis (F3) ruled out** | Primary care / diabetology; lifestyle + comorbidity treatment; re-assess with FIB-4 every 1–3 years |
| **8 – 12 kPa** | **Indeterminate** — between rule-out and rule-in | Intensify metabolic management; consider second test (ELF, MRE) or specialist opinion; repeat |
| **≥ 12 kPa** | **Advanced fibrosis (F3) ruled in** | **Hepatology referral.** Discuss MASH-targeted therapy (resmetirom if locally approved and non-cirrhotic F2–F3) |
| **≥ 15 kPa** | Possible compensated advanced chronic liver disease — **CSPH can no longer be ruled out on LSM alone** | Hepatology; assess for portal hypertension (Step 4) |
| **≥ 20 kPa** | High risk of varices | **Upper GI endoscopy** to screen for varices unless already starting NSBB |
| **≥ 25 kPa** | Clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) ruled in — **only if BMI < 30** | Hepatology; consider non-selective beta-blocker |

Other EASL-cited prognostic markers:

- LSM **≥ 16.6 kPa** predicted progression to cirrhosis (HR ~4) in advanced fibrosis cohorts
- LSM **≥ 30.7 kPa** predicted liver-related events (HR ~10)
- **LSM > 10 kPa with a rising trend** is an HCC risk factor (Table 8)

## Step 3 — Interpret CAP (steatosis grade)

| CAP (dB/m) | Steatosis grade (EASL Table 6) |
|---|---|
| **≥ 248** | **S1** — mild (< 10% hepatocytes) |
| **≥ 268** | **S2** — moderate (10–30%) |
| **≥ 280** | **S3** — severe (> 30%) |

**What CAP means and doesn't mean:**

- CAP **confirms steatosis** and gives a grade
- CAP **does not predict liver outcomes** (EASL marks its prognostic value as uncertain)
- A high CAP **does not mean fibrosis**; a low CAP **does not exclude it**
- Use CAP to **track response** to weight loss or therapy, not to decide referral
- MRI-PDFF is the non-invasive gold standard if precise fat quantification is needed

---

## Step 4 — Portal hypertension and varices (compensated advanced disease)

EASL adopts the **Baveno VII** criteria for MASLD:

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **LSM < 15 kPa AND platelets ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L** | **CSPH ruled out** |
| **LSM ≥ 20 kPa OR platelets < 150 × 10⁹/L** | **Endoscopy to screen for varices** (unless NSBB already indicated) |
| **LSM < 20 kPa AND platelets ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L** | Endoscopy can be safely skipped (< 5% missed varices needing treatment) |
| **LSM ≥ 25 kPa (BMI < 30 only)** | **CSPH ruled in** — consider NSBB |

**Obesity caveat:** the 25 kPa rule-in threshold **does not apply when BMI ≥ 30**. Evidence is insufficient to name the best rule-in test in obesity. The ANTICIPATE-NASH model (LSM + platelets + BMI) may help.

**Spleen stiffness:** promising but MASLD data are limited and cut-offs aren't defined — not for routine decisions yet.

---

## Step 5 — Combined score: FAST

If the report gives **FAST** (FibroScan-AST: LSM + CAP + AST) — targets "at-risk MASH" (steatohepatitis with ≥ F2):

- **≤ 0.35** → rules out at-risk MASH
- **≥ 0.67** → rules in at-risk MASH

---

## Step 6 — Compare with the previous scan

- **Use the same device and probe** for follow-up; method variability can exceed real change
- In longitudinal studies, LSM change of **±20%** (rise / stable / fall) tracked liver-related event risk
- A rise of **≥ 5.5 kPa (and ≥ 20%)** predicted progression to cirrhosis (HR ~2) in advanced fibrosis
- A single higher value **> 7.9 kPa** may not reproduce in about one-third — **confirm before escalating**
- CAP falling with weight loss is expected; it is a response marker, not an outcome marker

---

## Step 7 — Put it together in the report note

A good one-line summary:

> *"VCTE: LSM [X] kPa ([rule-out < 8 / indeterminate 8–12 / rule-in ≥ 12]), CAP [Y] dB/m ([S0–S3]), valid scan [yes/no], platelets [Z]. Interpretation: [advanced fibrosis excluded / indeterminate / likely advanced fibrosis]. CSPH [ruled out / not excluded / ruled in]. Plan: [re-test interval / second test / hepatology / endoscopy]."*

## Guardrails

- **Fibrosis, not fat, drives prognosis** — never refer or reassure on CAP alone
- **Do not interpret an invalid scan** — failed quality checks mean repeat or use another test
- **Obesity falsely raises LSM** — be cautious with borderline values at BMI ≥ 30, and do not use the 25 kPa CSPH rule-in above BMI 30
- **One borderline LSM is not a diagnosis** — VCTE variability is large; confirm values near cut-offs
- **Acute hepatitis, congestion, cholestasis and a recent meal raise LSM** — interpret in clinical context (practice point, not EASL-specified)
- **These are MASLD cut-offs** — viral hepatitis, ALD and cholestatic disease use different thresholds
- **LSM cannot diagnose steatohepatitis** (ballooning, lobular inflammation) — only biopsy can, and that rarely changes management
- **Platelets matter** — always read LSM together with the platelet count for portal hypertension decisions
- **Don't start HCC surveillance on LSM alone** below F3; do start it in cirrhosis
