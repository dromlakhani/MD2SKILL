# MASLD Fibrosis Risk Stratification (FIB-4 → Elastography)

Finds the minority of people with MASLD who have advanced fibrosis, because fibrosis stage — not steatosis — drives liver-related outcomes. The risk of liver-related events starts to rise at fibrosis stage F2.

---

## Step 1 — Decide whether to look for fibrosis

**Look for MASLD with fibrosis (strong recommendation)** if the adult has any of:

- **(A) Type 2 diabetes**
- **(B) Abdominal obesity plus ≥1 additional cardiometabolic risk factor**
- **(C) Abnormal / persistently elevated liver enzymes**

Also apply the pathway when **steatosis is found incidentally** on imaging.

**Consider case-finding (weak recommendation)** in anyone with cardiometabolic risk factors, abnormal liver enzymes, or radiological steatosis.

**Do NOT case-find** when life expectancy is set by something else — advanced age, active malignancy, advanced cardiovascular disease.

**Note:** first-degree relatives of people with advanced MASLD fibrosis have a 2–3× higher MASLD risk and ~12× higher advanced-fibrosis risk.

---

## Step 2 — Confirm it is MASLD and not something else

- Steatosis **plus ≥1 cardiometabolic risk factor**, **without harmful alcohol intake**
- Moderate alcohol intake with metabolic risk = **MetALD**; heavy intake = **ALD** — same fibrosis pathway applies, but alcohol is the target
- Exclude other causes when the picture doesn't fit (drug-induced, viral, autoimmune, monogenic)
- **Strong family history, early severe disease, or normal weight without metabolic triggers** → consider referral for genetic evaluation (next-generation sequencing)

Do **not** use ALT/AST alone to judge fibrosis — non-invasive scores are more accurate (strong recommendation).

---

## Step 3 — Calculate FIB-4 (first step)

**FIB-4 = (Age [years] × AST [U/L]) ÷ (Platelets [10⁹/L] × √ALT [U/L])**

| FIB-4 | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **< 1.3** | Low risk of advanced fibrosis | Lifestyle + manage comorbidities. **Re-assess every 1–3 years** |
| **1.3 – 2.67** | Indeterminate | **Option A:** go straight to elastography (preferred if FIB-4 close to 2.67 or high-risk condition). **Option B:** 1 year of intensified lifestyle + cardiometabolic management, then repeat FIB-4; if still ≥1.3 → elastography |
| **> 2.67** | High risk of advanced fibrosis | **Elastography** to confirm and stage; usually specialist involvement |

**Age adjustments:**

- **Age > 65 years:** use **2.0** as the lower cut-off instead of 1.3
- **Age < 35 years:** FIB-4 performs poorly — do not rely on it

**Known limits:** weakest in the 1.3–2.67 band, in the elderly, and in T2D; generates many false positives in low-prevalence settings. FIB-4 < 1.3 still **misses ~10%** of people with advanced fibrosis.

---

## Step 4 — Second step: elastography or a collagen-based blood test

**Preferred:** liver elastography — **VCTE (FibroScan)**; 2D-SWE or MRE are alternatives with their own cut-offs.

**Alternative or supplementary:** **ELF test** (enhanced liver fibrosis).

| Test | Rule-out advanced fibrosis (F3) | Rule-in advanced fibrosis (F3) |
|---|---|---|
| **VCTE liver stiffness** | **< 8 kPa** | **≥ 12 kPa** |
| 2D-SWE | < 8 kPa | ≥ 10.5 kPa |
| ELF | < 7.7 | ≥ 9.8 |
| MRE | — | F2 ≥ 3.14 kPa; F3 ≥ 3.53 kPa (F4 rule-out < 4.45 kPa) |

For reading a FibroScan report in detail, see `fibroscan-report-interpretation`.

---

## Step 5 — Route the patient

| Combined result | Route |
|---|---|
| FIB-4 low **or** LSM < 8 kPa | **Primary care / diabetology.** Lifestyle, weight loss, treat comorbidities (incretin-based therapy if T2D or obesity is an indication). Re-test FIB-4 every 1–3 years |
| LSM 8 – 12 kPa (indeterminate) | Treat metabolic drivers intensively; consider a second test (ELF, MRE, SWE) or specialist opinion; re-assess |
| LSM ≥ 12 kPa or ELF ≥ 9.8 | **Hepatology referral** — likely advanced fibrosis (F3–F4) |
| Signs of cirrhosis or portal hypertension | **Hepatology** — HCC and varices surveillance pathway |

**Treatment hooks once fibrosis is staged:**

- Non-cirrhotic MASH with **fibrosis stage ≥ F2** → consider **resmetirom** if locally approved (MASH-targeted therapy)
- **No MASH-targeted drug** is recommended at the cirrhotic stage
- Bariatric surgery is an option in MASLD with obesity

**HCC surveillance:**

- Fibrosis **< F3** → not recommended
- **F3** (non-cirrhotic) → may be considered on individual risk (FIB-4 > 3.25, LSM > 10 kPa rising, long-standing T2D/obesity, older age, alcohol, smoking)
- **Cirrhosis** → surveillance programme (6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP)

---

## Step 6 — Screen comorbidities at diagnosis (all MASLD patients)

Strong recommendation — assess at diagnosis and follow-up:

- **Obesity:** BMI, waist circumference, waist-to-height ratio; body composition if available
- **Glycaemia:** FPG, HbA1c, OGTT if needed; HOMA-IR may be considered if no T2D
- **Lipids:** fasting lipid panel; **Lp(a) once in a lifetime**
- **Kidney:** creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin
- **Cardiovascular:** blood pressure, hs-CRP, ferritin; NT-proBNP / echo if heart failure suspected
- **OSA, PCOS** where clinically relevant
- **Extrahepatic cancer screening** per standard guidelines

---

## Guardrails

- **Liver enzymes are not a fibrosis test** — normal ALT does not exclude advanced fibrosis
- **Always age-adjust FIB-4:** use 2.0 above age 65; don't trust it under 35
- **FIB-4 < 1.3 is not a lifetime clearance** — re-test every 1–3 years; ~10% of advanced fibrosis is missed
- **Indeterminate FIB-4 is not a diagnosis** — it needs a second test or a timed re-test
- **Don't case-find** where life expectancy is limited by other disease
- **Steatosis grade does not predict outcome** — fibrosis does. Don't reassure or alarm on steatosis alone
- **Liver biopsy is usually not needed** for management; it remains the only way to diagnose steatohepatitis and to rule out other liver disease
- **Class 2 obesity** may make ultrasound elastography unreliable — consider XL probe, MRE, or ELF
- **Use the same test and device** for follow-up — non-invasive tests vary between runs
