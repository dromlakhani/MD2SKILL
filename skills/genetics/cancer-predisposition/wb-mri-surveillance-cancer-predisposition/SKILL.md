# WB-MRI surveillance in cancer predisposition syndromes

Scope: selects the WB-MRI regimen for the syndromes the guideline names. It does not cover other organ-specific surveillance (colonoscopy, biochemistry, etc.). The guideline is silent on those, so refer to syndrome-specific guidance.

## STEP 1 - Identify the indication
Ask: is there a confirmed or suspected syndrome, or is the person asymptomatic with no known predisposition?
- LFS -> STEP 2
- CMMR-D -> STEP 3
- Hereditary paraganglioma-pheochromocytoma -> STEP 4
- Neurofibromatosis (NF1, NF2, schwannomatosis) -> STEP 5
- Another syndrome -> the guideline gives no recommendation. Say so and use the syndrome-specific guideline.
- No predisposition (general-population screening) -> STEP 6

## STEP 2 - Li-Fraumeni syndrome
- Interval: annual WB-MRI (AACR; 13-cohort, 578-participant review).
- Coverage: extend to the feet (soft-tissue tumour predisposition).
- Add-ons: contrast-enhanced brain MRI (post-contrast 3D T1W); in women, contrast-enhanced breast MRI.
- Start age: not specified in this guideline. State this; do not invent one.
- Action: order annual WB-MRI (vertex to feet) + contrast brain MRI (+ breast MRI if female).

## STEP 3 - CMMR-D
- Interval: annual WB-MRI.
- Start age: from age 6 years (Care for CMMRD / IBMMRDC consensus).
- Coverage and add-ons: not specified beyond the core protocol. Use the standard vertex to mid-thigh core protocol unless the local radiologist advises otherwise.
- Action: schedule the first WB-MRI at age 6, then yearly. Consider whether a child will tolerate a long, noisy scan (STEP 7).

## STEP 4 - Hereditary paraganglioma-pheochromocytoma
- Interval: biennial (every 2 years) WB-MRI (AACR).
- Start age, coverage, add-ons: not specified in this guideline.
- Action: order WB-MRI every 2 years; check the syndrome-specific guidance for start age.

## STEP 5 - Neurofibromatosis
- The guideline says acquisition should extend to the feet for NF (tumour burden on WB-MRI).
- Interval and start age: not specified.
- Action: request vertex-to-feet coverage; agree the interval locally.

## STEP 6 - General population or asymptomatic, no syndrome
- Screening remains debated. Studies (including one with 30,000 subjects) are too heterogeneous for firm conclusions.
- Action: do not present WB-MRI as an established screening test. Offer it only within a research or shared-decision context and counsel on incidental findings.

## STEP 7 - Protocol and logistics check
- Core protocol (under ~40 min): T1 Dixon, DWI (b50-100 and b800-1000), T2 without fat saturation, whole-spine sagittal T1/STIR, lung GRE; vertex to mid-thigh by default.
- Prefer 1.5 T if the patient has non-removable metallic implants.
- Prepare the patient: explain the long noisy exam, gown, void the bladder, breath-hold instructions.
- Action: send the request with syndrome, coverage (mid-thigh vs feet) and add-ons explicitly stated.

## Guardrails
- Never invent start ages or intervals for syndromes where the guideline gives none (LFS start age, paraganglioma start age, NF interval).
- Default coverage is vertex to mid-thigh. Feet are NOT included unless requested (LFS, NF).
- Brain MRI in LFS needs contrast; routine WB-MRI is non-contrast.
- Lung lesions below about 5 mm may be missed on WB-MRI; low-dose CT may be considered if clinically needed.
- Intervals here are from summarised guidelines. Check the current syndrome guideline before finalising.
- Do not use this for symptomatic staging of known cancer.

## Example
A 28-year-old woman with a germline TP53 variant, asymptomatic. Plan: annual WB-MRI vertex to feet + contrast-enhanced brain MRI + contrast-enhanced breast MRI. Note that the guideline gives no start age, so apply the LFS guideline for timing.
