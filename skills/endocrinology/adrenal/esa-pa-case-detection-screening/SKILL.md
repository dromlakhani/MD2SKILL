# Case Detection of Primary Aldosteronism: Who to Screen and How to Use the ARR

## STEP 1 — Gather Information
- BP history: values on three separate days, number and classes of antihypertensive drugs (diuretic included or not).
- Serum/plasma potassium (spontaneous or diuretic-induced hypokalemia).
- Adrenal incidentaloma on imaging; sleep apnea.
- Family history: early-onset hypertension or cerebrovascular accident at a young age (<40 years); first-degree relative with PA.
- Current medications affecting the ARR (Table 3): MR antagonists, amiloride, triamterene, K+-wasting diuretics, beta-blockers, central alpha-2 agonists, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, renin inhibitors, dihydropyridine CCBs; licorice; oral contraceptive/HRT status.
- Age, sex/menstrual phase, creatinine, dietary sodium.

## STEP 2 — Rule In / Rule Out (Who to Screen)
Rec 1.1: "We recommend case detection of primary aldosteronism (PA) in patients with" any of the following (1|⊕⊕○○):
- Sustained BP above 150/100 mm Hg on each of three measurements obtained on different days.
- Hypertension (BP >140/90 mm Hg) resistant to three conventional antihypertensive drugs (including a diuretic).
- Controlled BP (<140/90 mm Hg) on four or more antihypertensive drugs.
- Hypertension and spontaneous or diuretic-induced hypokalemia.
- Hypertension and adrenal incidentaloma.
- Hypertension and sleep apnea.
- Hypertension and a family history of early onset hypertension or cerebrovascular accident at a young age (<40 years).
- All hypertensive first-degree relatives of patients with PA.
If none apply, the guideline does not recommend case detection (selective, not universal, screening).

## STEP 3 — Classify or Stratify (Perform and Read the ARR)
Rec 1.2: "We recommend using the plasma aldosterone/renin ratio (ARR) to detect possible cases of PA in these patient groups." (1|⊕⊕⊕○)
Preparation (Table 4):
- Correct hypokalemia; aim for plasma K+ 4.0 mmol/L. Liberalize (not restrict) sodium intake.
- Withdraw for at least 4 weeks: spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride, triamterene, K+-wasting diuretics, licorice products.
- If results are not diagnostic and BP allows, withdraw for at least 2 weeks: beta-blockers, central alpha-2 agonists, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, renin inhibitors, dihydropyridine CCBs.
- Substitute agents with minimal ARR effect if needed (Table 5): verapamil slow-release 90-120 mg twice daily; hydralazine 10-12.5 mg twice daily (start verapamil first); prazosin 0.5-1 mg two or three times daily; doxazosin 1-2 mg once daily; terazosin 1-2 mg once daily.
- Do not withdraw oral contraceptives unless confident of alternative effective contraception.
Collection: midmorning, after the patient has been up for at least 2 hours and seated for 5-15 minutes; avoid stasis and hemolysis; keep sample at room temperature (not on ice).
Interpretation:
- Labs should report PAC, PRA/renin concentration, and the ARR individually.
- Cutoffs are assay-dependent (Table 6); the most commonly adopted cutoffs are 30 (PAC ng/dL, PRA ng/mL/h; 830 when PAC in SI units) and 750 (PAC pmol/L).
- Renin assays should measure as low as 0.2-0.3 ng/mL/h (DRC 2 mU/L).
- Very low renin can raise the ARR even when aldosterone is low; some investigators require PAC >15 ng/dL (410 pmol/L), but PA (especially BAH) can occur with PAC <15 ng/dL.

## STEP 4 — Decide
- Negative ARR, good conditions: PA not detected. Repeat the ARR if results are inconclusive, sampling conditions were suboptimal, or PA is strongly suspected clinically despite a negative screen.
- Positive ARR: Rec 2.1: "Instead of proceeding directly to subtype classification, we recommend that patients with a positive ARR undergo one or more confirmatory tests to definitively confirm or exclude the diagnosis" (1|⊕⊕○○). See esa-pa-interpret-sit, esa-pa-interpret-fst, esa-pa-interpret-oral-sodium-loading, esa-pa-interpret-cct.
- Exception: spontaneous hypokalemia, plasma renin below detection levels, plus PAC >20 ng/dL (550 pmol/L): "we suggest that there may be no need for further confirmatory testing" (2|⊕○○○). See esa-pa-skip-confirmatory-testing.

## Clinical Guardrails / Mimics / Pitfalls
- Hypokalemia is present in only a minority of PA (9 to 37%); normokalemia does not exclude PA and should not stop screening.
- The ARR is a detection test only, never a diagnosis.
- False positives (Table 3): beta-blockers, central agonists, NSAIDs, renin inhibitors (with PRA), advancing age (>65 years), premenopausal women (luteal phase, with DRC), renal impairment, PHA-2, sodium loading.
- False negatives: K+-wasting and K+-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, dihydropyridine CCBs, hypokalemia, sodium restriction, pregnancy, renovascular and malignant hypertension.
- Estrogen-containing medications may lower DRC and cause false-positive ARR when DRC is measured; screen at-risk women in the follicular phase if practicable.
- If an MR antagonist cannot be safely stopped in severe PA, testing can proceed as long as renin is suppressed.
- Complete cessation of all antihypertensives is usually unnecessary; if interfering agents cannot be withdrawn, perform the ARR and interpret in light of confounders.

## Concrete Clinical Example
A 52-year-old man has BP 152/96 mm Hg on amlodipine, lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide (resistant hypertension, three drugs including a diuretic). Potassium is 3.4 mmol/L. He meets Rec 1.1 criteria, so ARR testing is indicated. Potassium is supplemented toward 4.0 mmol/L, hydrochlorothiazide is withdrawn for at least 4 weeks, and verapamil slow-release is used for BP control. Midmorning seated sampling shows PAC 18 ng/dL and PRA 0.3 ng/mL/h (ARR 60, above the common cutoff of 30). Renin is detectable and his hypokalemia was diuretic-induced rather than spontaneous, so he proceeds to confirmatory testing rather than directly to subtype classification.
