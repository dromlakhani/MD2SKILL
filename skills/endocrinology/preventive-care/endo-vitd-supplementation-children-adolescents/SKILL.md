# Empiric Vitamin D Supplementation in Children and Adolescents (1 to 18 Years)

## STEP 1 — Gather Information
Confirm age is 1 to 18 years (the guideline did not address children aged 0-1 years; other guidelines already recommend empiric vitamin D in the first year of life). Confirm the child is generally healthy, without established indications for vitamin D treatment or 25(OH)D testing (eg, hypocalcemia). Assess current vitamin D sources: sun-safe outdoor activity, vitamin D-containing or fortified foods, and existing supplements or multivitamins. Note higher-risk features named by the guideline: limited sun exposure, dietary restrictions, high skin melanin content, and immigrant or refugee background.

## STEP 2 — Rule In / Rule Out
Rule in: generally healthy child or adolescent aged 1 to 18 years. Rule out (recommendation does not apply): age under 1 year; established indications for vitamin D treatment or 25(OH)D testing (eg, hypocalcemia) or underlying medical conditions, which need condition-specific management. The recommendation is not meant to replace the IOM Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) for vitamin D, which the panel treated as a baseline standard for all individuals.

## STEP 3 — Classify or Stratify
No 25(OH)D-based stratification: the panel found no clear evidence defining the optimal target 25(OH)D level for disease prevention in the populations considered, and suggests against routine 25(OH)D testing in all populations considered, in the absence of established indications. The panel judged supplementation may be especially prudent for those not expected to have adequate vitamin D via sun exposure and vitamin D-containing or fortified foods, and those for whom confidence is low that IOM DRIs are reliably achieved.

## STEP 4 — Decide
Recommendation 1: "In children and adolescents aged 1 to 18 years, we suggest empiric vitamin D supplementation to prevent nutritional rickets and potentially lower the risk of respiratory tract infections." (2 | ⊕⊕◯◯ — conditional, low certainty)
- Delivery (technical remark): empiric vitamin D may include daily intake of fortified foods, vitamin formulations that contain vitamin D, and/or a daily vitamin D supplement (pill or drops).
- Dose (technical remark): in included trials on respiratory tract infections in children, dosages ranged from 300 to 2000 IU (7.5 to 50 μg) daily equivalent; estimated weighted average approximately 1200 IU (30 μg) per day. The guideline states the optimal dose remains uncertain and makes no specific dose recommendation.
- Do not routinely test 25(OH)D before supplementing; the panel suggests against routine 25(OH)D testing in the absence of established indications.
- Conditional recommendation: net benefit may vary by individual circumstances; use shared decision-making consistent with the family's values and preferences.

## Clinical Guardrails / Mimics / Pitfalls
- No RCTs tested prevention of symptomatic rickets; benefit is inferred from historical and program data generalized to children with open growth plates at risk of rickets.
- Respiratory infection benefit is uncertain: any respiratory tract infection RR 0.94 (95% CI, 0.87-1.02); lower respiratory tract infection RR 0.93 (95% CI, 0.83-1.04). Most pediatric trials were conducted in Asia.
- Harms judged trivial; trials documented one case of symptomatic hypercalcemia in the vitamin D arm.
- A child with rickets, hypocalcemia, or another established indication is outside this recommendation; evaluate and treat, not empiric prevention.
- Supplement adherence barriers include dislike of drops, low priority, and belief that breastfeeding, outdoor play, or a varied diet is sufficient; in one trial, 95% of children agreed to supplements, suggesting supplements may be the most accepted formulation.

## Concrete Clinical Example
A healthy 7-year-old girl with dark complexion who recently immigrated, spends little time outdoors, and rarely eats fortified foods attends a well-child visit. Her parent asks for a vitamin D blood test. The clinician explains the guideline suggests against routine 25(OH)D testing in healthy children and instead suggests empiric vitamin D (2 | ⊕⊕◯◯) to prevent nutritional rickets and possibly reduce respiratory infections. After shared decision-making, they choose a daily vitamin D supplement, noting that pediatric trials used 300 to 2000 IU (7.5 to 50 μg) daily equivalent; no 25(OH)D test is ordered.
