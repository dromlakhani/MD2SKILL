# Time and Choose Postoperative Pituitary Imaging in Acromegaly

## STEP 1 — Gather Information
Confirm the patient has had pituitary surgery for acromegaly; record the date of surgery and calculate weeks elapsed; note any contraindication to MRI or lack of MRI availability; note whether a preoperative visual field defect was present; collect postoperative IGF-1 and GH results being used to judge remission.

## STEP 2 — Rule In / Rule Out
Rule in: post-surgical acromegaly patient needing assessment of residual tumor and adjacent structures. Rule out (defer): fewer than 12 weeks since surgery — the guideline states postoperative imaging "should be performed no sooner than 12 weeks after surgery to allow for involution of gel foam and fat packing."

## STEP 3 — Classify or Stratify
- MRI possible and available: MRI is the modality of choice.
- MRI contraindicated or unavailable: CT scan.
- Preoperative visual field defect: also repeat visual field testing.

## STEP 4 — Decide
- Rec 4.7: "We recommend performing an imaging study at least 12 weeks after surgery to visualize residual tumor and adjacent structures (1|⊕⊕⊕○)."
- Rec 4.7: "We suggest MRI as the imaging modality of choice followed by CT scan when MRI is contraindicated or unavailable. (2|⊕⊕○○)"
- Treat this scan as the new baseline image for follow-up assessment.
- In patients with preoperative visual field defects, repeat visual field testing.

## Clinical Guardrails / Mimics / Pitfalls
- Imaging earlier than 12 weeks can be confounded by gel foam and fat packing that has not yet involuted.
- Imaging complements, but does not replace, biochemical assessment: the guideline uses IGF-1 and GH measured at 12 weeks or later to judge remission.
- Visual field deficits may continue to improve up to 1 year after surgery; do not judge final visual outcome too early.
- Persistent disease after surgery is managed with medical therapy (Rec 5.1, 1|⊕⊕⊕⊕).

## Concrete Clinical Example
A 45-year-old man underwent transsphenoidal resection of a GH-secreting macroadenoma 6 weeks ago and asks for a scan now. Imaging is deferred until at least 12 weeks after surgery. MRI is contraindicated for him, so pituitary CT is ordered at 13 weeks to visualize residual tumor and adjacent structures, alongside IGF-1 and GH. Because he had a preoperative visual field defect, visual field testing is repeated. The CT becomes his new baseline for follow-up.
