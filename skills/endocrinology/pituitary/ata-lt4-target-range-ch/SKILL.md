# Levothyroxine Target Range in Central Hypothyroidism

## STEP 1 — Gather Information
- Pituitary/hypothalamic disease history (tumor, surgery, radiation), other pituitary hormone deficits.
- Serum fT4 and TSH. Draw fT4 before the daily L-T4 dose: a trial sampling 2 hours after ingestion found slightly elevated mean fT4, so "we recommend checking fT4 before the L-T4 dose."
- Adrenal status: has adrenal insufficiency (AI) been evaluated?
- Body weight (for μg/kg/d dosing), age, comorbidities, fracture risk.
- Estrogen status (oral estrogen, contraceptives, pregnancy), GH therapy, antiepileptic drugs (AEDs).

## STEP 2 — Rule In / Rule Out
- Rec 1.6: "An fT4 level below the laboratory reference range in conjunction with a low, normal, or mildly elevated TSH in the setting of pituitary disease usually confirms a CH diagnosis." (1|⊕⊕⊕⊕)
- Rec 1.7: with low-normal fT4 and suspected mild CH, "we suggest starting levothyroxine (L-T4) if suggestive symptoms are present or following fT4 levels over time and starting treatment if the fT4 level decreases by 20% or more." (2|⊕○○○)
- Rec 1.8: "We suggest against using dynamic TSH-secretion testing to diagnose CH." (2|⊕⊕⊕○)
- Rec 2.20: "We suggest evaluating patients with CH for AI before starting L-T4 therapy. If this is not feasible, clinicians should prescribe empiric GC therapy in patients with CH who are starting L-T4 therapy until there is a definitive evaluation for AI." (2|⊕⊕○○)
  Rationale: thyroid hormone accelerates cortisol clearance and could unmask AI and precipitate adrenal crisis.

## STEP 3 — Classify or Stratify
- Untreated CH: new start (after AI addressed).
- On L-T4, stable: titrate to fT4 target.
- On L-T4 with a change in modifiers:
  - Starting/adjusting GH: fT4 may fall (16-18% of treated CH patients developed low fT4 within 3-6 months of GH); monitor about 6 weeks after starting or adjusting GH.
  - Estrogen change: estrogen raised mean L-T4 requirement in CH from 1.3 to 1.8 μg/kg/d.
  - Pregnancy: requirements may rise.
  - Starting an AED.
- Adjust dose for age, estrogen status, comorbidities and clinical context, including potential risks of overtreatment.

## STEP 4 — Decide
- Rec 2.6: "We recommend L-T4 in doses sufficient to achieve serum fT4 levels in the mid to upper half of the reference range. Appropriate L-T4 doses in CH average 1.6 μg/kg/d, with dose adjustments based on clinical context, age, and fT4 levels." (1|⊕⊕⊕○)
- Rec 2.7: "We suggest against treating CH with L-T3, thyroid extracts, or other formulations of thyroid hormones." (2|⊕⊕○○)
- Rec 2.8: "We recommend against using serum TSH levels to adjust thyroid replacement dosing in patients with CH." (1|⊕⊕⊕○)
- Rec 2.22: monitor euthyroid GHD patients starting GH for CH and begin L-T4 if fT4 falls below range; "CH patients with GHD who are already receiving L-T4 may require increased L-T4 doses when they begin GH therapy to maintain fT4 levels within target ranges." (1|⊕⊕○○)
- Rec 2.24: "In patients with CH requiring changes in estrogen therapy, we recommend monitoring fT4 levels and adjusting L-T4 doses to maintain fT4 levels within target ranges." (1|⊕⊕⊕○)
- Rec 3.20: monitor "fT4 or total T4 levels every 4 – 6 weeks for women with CH who become pregnant"; they may require increased L-T4 doses. (1|⊕⊕○○)
- Rec 3.28: "checking fT4 at least 6 weeks after starting an AED and increasing L-T4 doses if fT4 levels decrease below the target range." (1|⊕⊕○○)
- Rec 3.7: "With preoperative CH, we recommend using L-T4 therapy before nonemergency surgery and throughout the perioperative period." (1|⊕⊕⊕○)
- Rec 2.29: "We suggest clinicians monitor L-T4 replacement ... and avoid over-replacement to reduce the risk of fractures." (2|⊕⊕○○)

## Clinical Guardrails / Mimics / Pitfalls
- Undetectable TSH is expected on adequate L-T4 in CH; do not read it as overtreatment, even if TSH was measurable at diagnosis.
- Do not start L-T4 before AI is excluded or covered with empiric glucocorticoid.
- Overtreatment: higher L-T4 doses and fT4 levels correlated with vertebral fracture risk in hypopituitary patients; adjust for age, estrogen status, comorbidities and clinical context.
- Untreated GHD can mask CH by keeping fT4 in range; recheck fT4 after GH starts.
- Treat CH before GH stimulation testing; CH can impair accurate GHD diagnosis (Rec 2.23, 2|⊕⊕○○).
- Patients on glucocorticoid therapy do not appear to need L-T4 dose adjustment.
- Rising fT3 has been proposed as an overtreatment marker, but this has not been validated.

## Concrete Clinical Example
A 70-kg man, 4 months after transsphenoidal surgery for a nonfunctioning macroadenoma, has fT4 below the reference range with low-normal TSH, consistent with CH. Adrenal insufficiency is confirmed and hydrocortisone is started first. L-T4 is then started at about 1.6 μg/kg/d (about 112 μg/d). Follow-up fT4, drawn before the morning dose, is in the low-normal range; the dose is increased to bring fT4 into the mid to upper half of the range. TSH stays undetectable and is not used to titrate. When GH replacement is started later, fT4 is rechecked about 6 weeks afterward and L-T4 is increased because fT4 has fallen below target.
