# Awiqli (Insulin Icodec) Prescribing Guide

Once-weekly basal insulin analog. Binds albumin and releases slowly — half-life about one week, glucose-lowering effect covers the full week.

---

## Step 1 — Confirm the patient fits the label

**Indicated:** adults with **type 2 diabetes**, as an adjunct to diet and exercise.

**Not established / not labelled for:**
- **Type 1 diabetes** — the US label covers type 2 only
- **Children** — safety and effectiveness not established
- **Insulin pump, IV or IM use** — subcutaneous pen only

**Contraindicated:**
- **During an episode of hypoglycaemia**
- **Hypersensitivity** to insulin icodec or excipients (anaphylaxis has occurred)

---

## Step 2 — Choose the starting dose

| Patient | Start |
|---|---|
| **Insulin-naive** | **70 units once weekly**, same day each week |
| **On daily basal insulin** | Use `awiqli-switch-from-daily-basal` (week 1 loading dose, then week 2 dose) |

1 unit of icodec = 1 international unit of human insulin.

**Concomitant drugs at start:** in the trials, **sulfonylureas and glinides were stopped** (Trials A, C) **or reduced by about 50%** (Trial B) when icodec was started. Plan this to limit hypoglycaemia. Other agents (metformin, SGLT2i, DPP-4i, GLP-1 RA) were continued.

---

## Step 3 — Titrate

- Adjust weekly based on **fasting glucose, metabolic needs and glycaemic goal**
- The label gives **no fixed titration algorithm**. In the pivotal trials the dose was titrated in **20-unit steps** toward a **fasting glucose of 80–130 mg/dL**, using the mean of the last 3 fasting values
- **Steady state takes 2–3 weeks** (with the one-time week 1 extra dose when switching) or **3–4 weeks** without it — don't over-titrate early
- **Do not adjust for acute illness or short-term changes in diet or activity** — the long half-life makes weekly dose changes too slow. Manage with glucose intake or changes to other glucose-lowering drugs instead

---

## Step 4 — Administer

- **Subcutaneous, once weekly, any day, same day each week**
- **Thigh, upper arm or abdomen**; rotate sites within a region (lipodystrophy, cutaneous amyloidosis); never inject into lumpy areas
- Pen: **U-700 (700 units/mL)**, dials in **10-unit steps**, **up to 700 units per injection**. Doses above 700 units → **split into two injections** (e.g. 1,050 = 700 + 350)
- Pens: 3 mL (2,100 units), 1.5 mL (1,050 units), 1 mL (700 units, sample only)
- **Never** transfer to a syringe, mix with other insulin, use IV/IM or in a pump
- **Never share a pen**, even with a new needle
- Check the label before every injection; visually verify the dialled dose. Use caution in visual impairment (relying on clicks)

---

## Step 5 — Missed dose

| Time since missed dose | Action |
|---|---|
| **≤4 days** | Give as soon as possible; then resume once weekly, **one week from the day it was given** |
| **>4 days** | **Skip it**; give the next dose on the regular day |

Increase glucose monitoring after a missed dose.

---

## Step 6 — Special populations

| Group | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Elderly (≥65)** | No efficacy/safety difference in trials, but **conservative starting dose, increments and maintenance**; hypoglycaemia may be harder to recognise; monitor more often |
| **Renal impairment incl. ESRD/dialysis** | No PK change; **no specific dose adjustment**, but intensify monitoring and individualise |
| **Hepatic impairment** (mild–severe) | No PK change; **no specific dose adjustment**, but intensify monitoring and individualise |
| **Pregnancy** | **No human data.** Animal studies showed no direct developmental harm. Poorly controlled diabetes itself carries major risk — weigh carefully |
| **Lactation** | No data on milk transfer or infant effects |
| **Body weight** | Exposure falls as weight rises (40–160 kg); dose by glucose response |

---

## Step 7 — Storage

| | Refrigerated 2–8 °C | Room temperature <30 °C |
|---|---|---|
| **Unopened** | Until expiry | 12 weeks |
| **In use** | 12 weeks | 12 weeks |

Total time at room temperature (including in use) **must not exceed 12 weeks**. **Do not freeze**; don't store next to the cooling element; discard if frozen.

---

## Step 8 — Key efficacy and side effects (for counselling)

- **Insulin-naive vs glargine U-100 (Trial A, 52 weeks):** HbA1c −1.51% vs −1.33% (difference −0.18%); time in range 71% vs 67%
- **Insulin-naive vs degludec (Trial B, 26 weeks):** difference −0.22% in HbA1c
- **Basal-bolus vs glargine (Trial D):** non-inferior HbA1c (difference 0.02%)
- **Weight gain** 1.4–2.8 kg; **peripheral oedema** 1.2%; **injection-site reactions** 1.8%; **hypersensitivity** 0.4%
- **Anti-drug antibodies** are common (about 70–80%) but had no identified effect on safety or efficacy over 26 weeks

---

## Guardrails

- **Type 2 adults only** on the US label
- **Weekly, not daily** — dosing-frequency errors have caused hospitalisation
- **Stop or halve sulfonylureas/glinides at the start**
- **Don't chase illness or diet changes with the weekly dose** — adjust other therapy
- **Split doses above 700 units**
- **Expect 3–4 weeks to steady state** without a loading dose — titrate patiently
- See `awiqli-hypoglycaemia-and-safety` for hypoglycaemia risk, drug interactions and hypokalaemia
