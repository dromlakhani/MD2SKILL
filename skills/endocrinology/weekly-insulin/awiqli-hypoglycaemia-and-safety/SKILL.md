# Awiqli (Insulin Icodec) — Hypoglycaemia & Safety

Hypoglycaemia is the commonest adverse reaction. With a one-week half-life, **the drug can't be "switched off"** — a weekly dose that is too high keeps acting for days.

---

## Step 1 — Prevent medication errors

Serious hypoglycaemia needing hospitalisation has occurred from:
- **Mix-ups** with other insulins or other once-weekly injectables (e.g. weekly GLP-1 RAs)
- **Wrong dose selection** (U-700 — 700 units/mL)
- **Dosing-frequency errors** (taking it daily instead of weekly)

**Prevent by:**
- Training patient and carer on selecting the weekly dose before starting
- Checking the product label before every injection
- Visually confirming the dialled units on the dose counter
- **Never** drawing it into a syringe
- Not dialling 700 units unless that is the dose

---

## Step 2 — Identify higher hypoglycaemia risk

Risk rises with:
- **Tighter glycaemic targets**
- **Changes in meal pattern** (content, timing), **physical activity**, or **co-medications**
- **Renal or hepatic impairment**
- **Long-standing diabetes, autonomic neuropathy, beta-blockers, recurrent hypoglycaemia** — fewer warning symptoms
- **Elderly** — harder to recognise; use conservative dosing
- **Concomitant sulfonylureas/glinides or bolus insulin**
- **First several weeks** after starting or after dose escalation

**Trial rates of level 2 hypoglycaemia (<54 mg/dL):**
- Insulin-naive trials: **8.9–14.1%**
- **Basal-bolus (with insulin aspart): 50.9%**; level 3 (severe) 1.4%

**Higher-risk patients:** monitor glucose more often; consider CGM.

---

## Step 3 — Manage hypoglycaemia

- **Mild:** oral glucose
- **Severe (coma, seizure, neurological impairment):** glucagon or concentrated IV glucose
- **Recovery may be delayed** compared with shorter-acting insulins — after apparent recovery, **continue observation and extra carbohydrate** to prevent recurrence
- **Check and correct potassium** (insulin shifts K⁺ into cells)
- Then review: reduce the next weekly dose, adjust meals/activity, cut sulfonylurea or bolus insulin
- **Do not give Awiqli during a hypoglycaemic episode** (contraindicated)

---

## Step 4 — Acute illness and short-term changes

- **Do not adjust the weekly dose** for acute illness or short-term changes in activity or diet — the long half-life makes this ineffective and unsafe
- Instead, manage with **glucose intake** or **changes to other glucose-lowering drugs**
- Dose changes may be needed with lasting changes in **renal or hepatic function**

---

## Step 5 — Drug interactions

| Effect | Drugs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **May increase hypoglycaemia risk** | Other antidiabetic drugs (incl. GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i, SGLT2i), ACEi, ARBs, disopyramide, fibrates, fluoxetine, MAOIs, pentoxifylline, pramlintide, salicylates, somatostatin analogues (octreotide), sulfonamide antibiotics | Lower dose, monitor more |
| **May reduce glucose-lowering effect** | Atypical antipsychotics (olanzapine, clozapine), corticosteroids, danazol, diuretics, estrogens, glucagon, isoniazid, niacin, oral contraceptives, phenothiazines, progestogens, protease inhibitors, somatropin, sympathomimetics (albuterol, epinephrine, terbutaline), thyroid hormones | Higher dose, monitor more |
| **May raise or lower glucose** | Alcohol, beta-blockers, clonidine, lithium; pentamidine (hypoglycaemia then hyperglycaemia) | Adjust and monitor |
| **May mask hypoglycaemia symptoms** | Beta-blockers, clonidine, guanethidine, reserpine | Monitor more often |

**Steroid courses (practice point, not stated in the label):** because the label advises against adjusting the weekly dose for short-term changes, cover steroid-induced hyperglycaemia with other agents (e.g. prandial/correction insulin) rather than a large weekly increase.

---

## Step 6 — Other safety issues

| Issue | Action |
|---|---|
| **Hypokalaemia** | All insulins shift K⁺ intracellularly; untreated it can cause paralysis, arrhythmia, death. Monitor K⁺ in at-risk patients (K-lowering drugs, drugs sensitive to K⁺) |
| **Hypersensitivity** (0.4%; anaphylaxis possible) | Stop Awiqli, treat, monitor until resolved |
| **Thiazolidinedione (pioglitazone)** | Fluid retention and heart failure risk with insulin. Watch for HF; reduce or stop the TZD if HF develops. Peripheral oedema 1.2% with icodec alone |
| **Injection into lipodystrophy/amyloidosis** | Causes hyperglycaemia; sudden switch to normal skin can cause hypoglycaemia — change site and monitor closely |
| **Rapid glucose improvement** | Transient refraction change, worsening retinopathy, acute painful neuropathy can occur |
| **Pen sharing** | Never — blood-borne infection risk |

---

## Step 7 — Overdose

Excess insulin → **prolonged** severe hypoglycaemia and hypokalaemia. Treat as Step 3; expect a long course given the one-week half-life; correct potassium.

---

## Guardrails

- **Weekly dosing errors are the big danger** — reinforce at every visit
- **Lows can last longer** than with daily insulin — observe and give carbohydrate after recovery
- **Don't change the weekly dose for short-term illness** — use other drugs
- **Basal-bolus patients have the highest hypoglycaemia rates** — reduce mealtime insulin proactively
- **Check potassium** in severe hypoglycaemia and in patients on K-lowering drugs
- **Beta-blockers mask symptoms** — monitor glucose more often
