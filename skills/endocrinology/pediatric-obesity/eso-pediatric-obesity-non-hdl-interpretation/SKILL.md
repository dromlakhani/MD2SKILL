# Interpret Non-HDL Cholesterol in Children/Adolescents With Overweight or Obesity

## STEP 1 — Gather Information
- Age, sex, and BMI percentile (CDC normative charts); confirm BMI ≥85th percentile.
- Lipid panel: total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, triglycerides; non-HDL cholesterol = total cholesterol − HDL cholesterol.
- Fasting status: Table 2 lists dyslipidaemia screening as "Fasting lipids", but notes non-HDL cholesterol "can be nonfasting".
- Units: Table 2 cut-offs are in mg/dL; to convert cholesterol mg/dL to mmol/L, multiply by 0.0259 (triglycerides: 0.0113).
- Family history: dyslipidaemia, premature CVD events/deaths (such as heart attacks or strokes), T2DM, hypertension.

## STEP 2 — Rule In / Rule Out
- Rule in: child or adolescent with BMI ≥85th percentile. Guideline 1.6: "We recommend that children or adolescents with a BMI ≥85th percentile be evaluated for potential comorbidities (see Table 2 and Fig. 1)." (1|⊕⊕⊕○, strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence)
- Dyslipidaemia is one of the listed comorbidities of pediatric overweight and obesity.
- Rule out: BMI <85th percentile falls outside this recommendation.
- If the sample was nonfasting, only the non-HDL value is labelled as valid nonfasting; the other lipid cut-offs sit under "Fasting lipids".

## STEP 3 — Classify or Stratify
Non-HDL cholesterol (Table 2, from the Expert Panel Summary Report; can be nonfasting):
| Category | Non-HDL cholesterol (mg/dL) |
|---|---|
| Acceptable | <120 |
| Borderline high | 120–144 |
| High | ≥145 |

Interpret together with the other Table 2 lipid values (mg/dL):
- Total cholesterol: <170 acceptable; 170–199 borderline high; ≥200 high.
- LDL cholesterol: <110 acceptable; 110–129 borderline high; ≥130 high.
- HDL cholesterol: <40 low; 40–45 borderline low; >45 acceptable.
- Triglycerides, 0–9 y: <75 acceptable; 75–99 borderline high; ≥100 high.
- Triglycerides, 10–19 y: <90 acceptable; 90–129 borderline high; ≥130 high.

## STEP 4 — Decide
- Acceptable non-HDL (<120 mg/dL): no dyslipidaemia flag from this value; complete the rest of Table 2 comorbidity screening (glycaemia, BP, ALT, PCOS features, sleep apnea, psychiatric history).
- Borderline high (120–144 mg/dL) or high (≥145 mg/dL): flag on the Table 2 dyslipidaemia screen and report the category; if the sample was nonfasting, triglycerides and LDL need a fasting sample to be classified, because Table 2 lists them under "Fasting lipids".
- Classify each lipid component separately and report each category.
- The guideline's aim is to "identify comorbidities early and initiate appropriate management"; it does not set lipid-lowering drug thresholds in this section, so do not infer them from these cut-offs.

## Clinical Guardrails / Mimics / Pitfalls
- Use the Table 2 pediatric values (non-HDL high is ≥145 mg/dL).
- Triglyceride cut-offs are age-banded (0–9 y vs 10–19 y); use the correct band.
- HDL runs the other way: <40 mg/dL is low, 40–45 borderline low.
- Non-HDL is the only Table 2 lipid value labelled "can be nonfasting"; do not classify nonfasting triglycerides with the fasting cut-offs.
- Guideline 1.7: "We recommend against measuring insulin concentrations when evaluating children or adolescents for obesity." (1|⊕⊕⊕○) Do not add fasting insulin to the work-up.
- Guideline 1.5: "We recommend against routine laboratory evaluations for endocrine etiologies of pediatric obesity unless the patient's stature and/or height velocity are attenuated (assessed in relationship to genetic/familial potential and pubertal stage)." (1|⊕⊕⊕○) Dyslipidaemia alone is not a trigger for that work-up.
- The greater the severity of obesity, the higher the risk of cardiometabolic risk factors, particularly among boys; comorbidities may be asymptomatic/subclinical, so screen regardless of symptoms.

## Concrete Clinical Example
A 13-year-old boy with BMI at the 97th percentile attends clinic after lunch. Nonfasting labs: total cholesterol 196 mg/dL, HDL 38 mg/dL. Non-HDL = 196 − 38 = 158 mg/dL, which is high (≥145). HDL 38 mg/dL is low (<40); total cholesterol 196 mg/dL is borderline high (170–199). The dyslipidaemia screen is flagged and each category reported. Because the sample was nonfasting, triglycerides (10–19 y band, ≥130 high) and LDL (≥130 high) need a fasting sample before they can be classified. The rest of Table 2 screening (HbA1c/fasting glucose, BP percentile, ALT) is completed; fasting insulin is not ordered.

**Source:** Pediatric Obesity—Assessment, Treatment, and Prevention: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline, Endocrine Society, 2017, DOI:10.1210/jc.2016-2573
