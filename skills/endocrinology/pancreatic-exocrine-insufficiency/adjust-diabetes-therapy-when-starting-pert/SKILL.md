# Adjust diabetes treatment and glucose monitoring when starting PERT

Purpose: before and after PERT is started in a patient with diabetes, plan glucose surveillance and decide in which direction to adjust glucose-lowering drugs. PERT restores carbohydrate digestion, so postprandial glucose can rise. Better overall metabolic status may also improve insulin sensitivity, so hypoglycaemia is possible.

## STEP 1. Gather baseline before the first dose
- Record: diabetes type, current regimen (insulin type/doses, sulfonylurea or other secretagogue, other agents), recent HbA1c, hypoglycaemia history, CGM or SMBG availability.
- Record baseline HbA1c (the guideline asks for it at baseline).
- Confirm PERT is actually indicated (FE-1 <200 ug/g, or borderline FE-1 100-200 with a suggestive clinical picture). If not, stop here and use the PEI diagnosis skill.
- Action: baseline documented, then proceed.

## STEP 2. Stratify the glycaemic risk of starting PERT
- On insulin or a sulfonylurea/secretagogue: both directions are possible, so plan close surveillance and a pre-agreed adjustment plan.
- Type 1 diabetes: CGM with real-time alerts is especially useful.
- On diet or agents with low hypoglycaemia risk: the main expected issue is a higher postprandial glucose.
- Action: the higher-risk groups get CGM; if CGM is unavailable, use intensified SMBG (fasting plus postprandial). The guideline does not specify SMBG frequency; set it clinically.

## STEP 3. Counsel the patient before starting
- Tell them glucose patterns will change after PERT starts, and that sugars may rise or fall.
- Teach hypoglycaemia recognition and what to do, and when to call.
- Enzymes go at the START of each fat-containing meal or snack. Do NOT advise fat restriction. Timing errors also change glucose patterns.
- Action: counselling documented; patient has a contact route.

## STEP 4. Monitor in the first 2-4 weeks
- Use CGM (preferred) for the first 2-4 weeks. Review Time in Range, Time Above Range, Time Below Range, mean glucose, nocturnal hypoglycaemia, glucose variability and hypoglycaemic events.
- Review alongside fasting and postprandial glucose.
- Action: review at the 2-4 week point (same visit as PERT response check) and sooner if alerts or events occur.

## STEP 5. Decide the adjustment
| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Postprandial/overall hyperglycaemia | Consider increasing mealtime insulin or adjusting pre-meal secretagogues |
| Hypoglycaemia (esp. nocturnal) or TBR concern | Cautiously reduce insulin; discontinue sulfonylurea if enhanced insulin sensitivity is suspected |
| Stable, in range | No change; continue monitoring |
- Titrate proactively rather than waiting for HbA1c. Involve endocrinology and gastroenterology jointly.
- Specific insulin dose-change percentages are NOT given in the guideline; use local protocols and clinical judgement.

## STEP 6. Reassess at 3 months
- Repeat HbA1c 3 months after PERT start and compare with baseline; continue SMBG as complementary data.
- Target is individualised per ADA (general benchmark <7% / 53 mmol/mol), adjusted for age, comorbidity and hypoglycaemia risk.
- Action: if HbA1c is above individual target, re-titrate therapy and re-check PERT dose and technique; if improved, continue and de-escalate drugs as needed.

## Guardrails
- Do not stop or withhold PERT for a post-start glucose rise. The guideline reports about 19% have mild hyperglycaemia, some need more insulin, yet net postprandial glucose and HbA1c improve. No DKA or hyperglycaemia-related hospitalisation has been reported after PERT.
- Do not forget hypoglycaemia: reduce sulfonylureas/insulin if sensitivity improves.
- Erratic glucose after PERT with persistent symptoms: first check enzyme timing (start of meal), adherence, dose adequacy, and snacks without enzymes, then consider SIBO, before blaming the drug.
- Do not use repeat FE-1 to judge response; judge by symptoms, weight, nutritional markers and glycaemia.
- Poor glycaemic control is itself linked to lower FE-1; unexplained poor control despite therapy is a reason to suspect PEI.
- Evidence is expert consensus, not GRADE-graded; the 19% figure comes from one real-life case-control study.

## Example
A 58-year-old man with T2DM for 14 years, on basal-bolus insulin plus glimepiride, with HbA1c 8.4%, steatorrhoea, weight loss and FE-1 120 ug/g, is started on PERT 50,000 lipase units with main meals. Plan: baseline HbA1c recorded; CGM for 2-4 weeks; counsel on hypoglycaemia; review at 2-4 weeks. If TAR is high after meals, consider raising mealtime insulin; if nocturnal lows appear, cautiously cut insulin and stop glimepiride. Repeat HbA1c at 3 months.
