# Start and titrate PERT in diabetes with PEI

## STEP 1: Decide whether to start PERT
Gather: FE-1 result (ug/g), symptoms (steatorrhoea, bloating, foul stools, weight loss, abdominal pain), fat-soluble vitamin status, unexplained poor glycaemic control.
- FE-1 <200 ug/g -> START PERT (severe <100, moderate 100-200). Same protocol for T1DM, T2DM and pancreatic disease.
- FE-1 100-200 AND clinical picture consistent -> START empirically; treat as a therapeutic trial (Step 6).
- FE-1 >200 -> PERT not indicated on FE-1 alone; look for another cause (SIBO, coeliac, IBD).
- Also consider with documented fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, unexplained malnutrition/weight loss or persistent steatorrhoea.
- FE-1 invalid during acute diarrhoea or with intestinal inflammation: do not rely on it; retest when settled.

## STEP 2: Choose the formulation
- Prefer enteric-coated microsphere or minimicrosphere products (particle size <2 mm; 0.7-1.6 mm optimal).
- Avoid non-enteric-coated preparations.
- Keep to one brand once stable (products differ in bioavailability). If a switch is needed, warn the patient and monitor more closely.

## STEP 3: Choose the starting dose
Adult:
- Main meal: 25,000-50,000 lipase units.
- Snack: 10,000-25,000 units.
- Mild-moderate PEI or outpatient setting: may start empirically at 10,000 units per meal and titrate.
Child (weight-based): 1,000-4,000 units/kg/meal, not above adult maximum, and total <=10,000 units/kg/day. Under 4 years the guideline says about 1,000 units/kg; over 4 years about 500 units/kg. Granules go in acidic puree (pH <4.5, e.g. applesauce), swallowed at once, never crushed or chewed.
-> Prescribe a dose per main meal AND per snack.

## STEP 4: Teach administration (before any escalation)
- Take at the START of every meal or snack containing fat; do not take more than 30 min before or after.
- Large meal: half at the start, half midway.
- Swallow capsules whole with cool, non-alkaline fluid; avoid hot drinks and antacids.
- Do NOT restrict dietary fat; ensure adequate calories; avoid very high fibre (>25 g/day).
- Never skip enzymes with fat-containing snacks (a frequent error).
-> Document that the patient can state timing and the fat advice.

## STEP 5: Prepare glycaemic safety plan
PERT restores carbohydrate digestion, so postprandial glucose may rise (mild hyperglycaemia in ~19%). Improved insulin sensitivity can cause hypoglycaemia.
- Warn the patient that glucose patterns will change.
- Intensive monitoring for the first 2-4 weeks, ideally CGM (TIR/TAR/TBR); otherwise SMBG.
- Hyperglycaemia: consider more mealtime insulin or adjusting pre-meal secretagogues.
- Hypoglycaemia: cautiously reduce insulin or stop sulfonylureas.
- HbA1c at baseline and 3 months.

## STEP 6: Review at 2-4 weeks and titrate
Response = symptom improvement, weight stabilisation/gain, better nutritional markers (and glycaemia).
- Responding -> continue; reinforce education regularly.
- Not responding -> check in order: technique and timing, adherence, underdosing -> then escalate by 25,000-50,000 units per meal.
- Empiric trial (borderline FE-1): response supports PEI and justifies continuing; no response -> reconsider the diagnosis.
- Patients may self-titrate to meal size (like insulin).

## STEP 7: Refractory or high-dose PEI
- Usual maximum 75,000-100,000 units per meal.
- Persistent symptoms at 50,000-75,000/meal with verified adherence and technique = refractory: exclude SIBO, coeliac disease, IBD; consider switching formulation.
- Add a PPI (omeprazole 20-40 mg or pantoprazole 40 mg daily, 30-60 min before first meal); reassess at 2-4 weeks.
- Above 100,000 units/meal: only after these exclusions, with careful monitoring (constipation, hyperuricaemia).
- Progressive weight loss despite therapy: investigate, including malignancy.

## Guardrails
- Do not repeat FE-1 to monitor treatment; it measures native secretion, not enzyme adequacy.
- Underdosing is the commonest cause of failure (improvement 87% at recommended doses vs 50% underdosed).
- Fix timing errors before raising the dose.
- Do not tell patients to cut fat.
- PPIs are second-line only; the long-term risks are C. difficile, low Mg, B12 malabsorption and fractures, so reassess the need periodically.
- Start fat-soluble vitamin replacement at the same time as PERT if deficient (see REFERENCE.md); check B12, especially on metformin.
- SIBO mimics PEI; breath test and antibiotics, not PERT, treat it.
- The guideline gives no brand names, no max dose for children beyond adult maximum, and no stopping criteria.

## Example
52-year-old man, T2DM for 14 years on metformin and a sulfonylurea, with bloating, foul stools and 4 kg weight loss. FE-1 150 ug/g. Start 25,000 units with meals and 10,000 with snacks, enteric-coated minimicrospheres, at the start of the meal, no fat restriction. CGM for 2-4 weeks and stop the sulfonylurea if hypoglycaemia occurs. At 3 weeks symptoms persist but he takes the capsule after eating: correct the timing first, then escalate by 25,000 if still symptomatic.
