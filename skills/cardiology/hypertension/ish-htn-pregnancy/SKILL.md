# Hypertension in Pregnancy (ISH 2026)

Hypertension in pregnancy = **BP ≥140/90 mmHg**; affects 5–10% of pregnancies. Maternal risks: abruption, stroke, organ failure, DIC, eclampsia. Fetal risks: growth restriction, preterm birth, intrauterine death.

---

## Step 1 — Classify the disorder

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Pre-existing hypertension** | Before pregnancy, or diagnosed **<20 weeks**, persisting **>12 weeks postpartum** (± proteinuria) |
| **Gestational hypertension** | Develops **≥20 weeks**, **no proteinuria**, resolves within 12 weeks postpartum |
| **Preeclampsia** | Hypertension **≥20 weeks** with **proteinuria or other maternal organ dysfunction or uteroplacental dysfunction** |
| **Eclampsia** | Preeclampsia with **seizures** — immediate treatment and consider prompt delivery |

**Preeclampsia risk factors:** pre-existing hypertension, previous preeclampsia, pre-existing diabetes, CKD, nulliparity, multifetal pregnancy, autoimmune disease, assisted reproduction, obesity, maternal age >40.

**Preeclampsia warning symptoms:** severe headache, visual disturbance, abdominal pain, nausea/vomiting, reduced urine output, **HELLP** (haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets).

---

## Step 2 — Investigate (ESSENTIAL)

- **FBC, urinalysis, creatinine, uric acid, liver enzymes**
- **Proteinuria:** early pregnancy (pre-existing renal disease) and second half (preeclampsia). Positive dipstick → **UACR or protein-to-creatinine ratio**

---

## Step 3 — Measure BP correctly

- **ESSENTIAL:** sOBP with calibrated manual auscultatory device, or automated upper-arm device **validated specifically in pregnancy and preeclampsia** (stridebp.org)
- **OPTIMAL:** home or ambulatory BP to detect white-coat and masked hypertension; home monitoring with pregnancy-validated devices

---

## Step 4 — Treat by BP level (ESSENTIAL)

**Target sOBP: <140/90 mmHg — but avoid BP that is too low.**

| sOBP | Action |
|---|---|
| **140–159 / 90–109** | **Start first-line monotherapy:** **labetalol** or **long-acting nifedipine**. Alternative: **methyldopa** (raises post-partum depression risk). Also acceptable (less data): metoprolol, bisoprolol, felodipine, nitrendipine, amlodipine, isradipine |
| **>160/110** | **Emergency — hospitalise.** Lower BP to **<160/105 within 2–3 h** with **IV labetalol** (or nicardipine, esmolol, hydralazine, urapidil), oral methyldopa, nifedipine or nicardipine. **Consider delivery** (cures preeclampsia, but risk persists early postpartum) |
| **Preeclampsia or severe hypertension** | **Hospitalise** |

**CONTRAINDICATED:** **ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors** — adverse fetal and neonatal outcomes.

For severe preeclampsia, eclampsia or HELLP: SBP <160 and DBP <105 immediately with **labetalol or nicardipine + magnesium sulphate** (see `ish-htn-emergency`).

---

## Step 5 — Postpartum and breastfeeding

- **Monitor BP daily on days 3–7 postpartum**
- **Postpartum:** any recommended drug **except methyldopa** (post-partum depression)
- **Breastfeeding:** **enalapril, long-acting nifedipine, amlodipine** preferred; beta-blockers may be used; **ARB data limited** — check prescribing information

---

## Step 6 — Long-term follow-up

Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy raise later risk of **hypertension, stroke, ischaemic heart disease and CKD** (and count as a CV risk factor — see `ish-htn-risk-assessment-workup`).

- Counsel on future pregnancies
- **Annual BP and renal function check**
- Lifestyle changes

---

## Step 7 — Prevent preeclampsia (ESSENTIAL)

- **Low-to-moderate intensity exercise** unless contraindicated
- **Women at increased risk:** **aspirin 100–150 mg from weeks 12–36**
- **Low dietary calcium (<600 mg/day):** **oral calcium ~1 g/day**

---

## Guardrails

- **Never use ACEi, ARB or renin inhibitors in pregnancy** — switch before or as soon as pregnancy is known
- **Avoid methyldopa postpartum** — depression risk
- **Don't over-lower BP** — target <140/90 but avoid hypotension (placental perfusion)
- **≥160/110 is an emergency** — hospitalise and lower within 2–3 h
- **Use devices validated for pregnancy** — many standard devices are not
- **Preeclampsia can develop without proteinuria** — organ dysfunction or uteroplacental dysfunction also count
- **Start aspirin by week 12** in high-risk women, not late
- **Arrange lifelong follow-up** — previous hypertensive pregnancy is a CV risk factor
