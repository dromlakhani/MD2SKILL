# Hypertension Workup & Cardiovascular Risk (ISH 2026)

Assess total cardiovascular (CV) risk in every person with hypertension. Risk decides whether drug treatment starts immediately or after 3 months of lifestyle change.

---

## Step 1 — History (ESSENTIAL)

- **Hypertension history:** onset, duration, previous BP, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, previous drugs, side effects, adherence
- **Drugs/substances that raise BP:** NSAIDs, combined OCP, SNRIs/MAOIs/tricyclics, calcineurin inhibitors, decongestants, stimulants, ADHD drugs, glucocorticoids, EPO, VEGF/tyrosine kinase inhibitors, triptans, liquorice, ephedra, St John's wort, excess caffeine/energy drinks
- **CV risk factors** (Step 4)
- **Symptoms of organ damage:** chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, claudication, oedema, headache, blurred vision, nocturia, haematuria, dizziness
- **Symptoms of secondary hypertension** (see `ish-htn-resistant-and-secondary`)
- **Lifestyle:** diet, salt, alcohol, smoking, physical activity, sleep
- **Family history**

---

## Step 2 — Examination (ESSENTIAL)

- **Circulation and heart:** pulse rate/rhythm, JVP, apex beat, extra heart sounds, basal crackles, oedema, carotid/abdominal/femoral bruits, lower-limb pulses, radio-femoral delay
- **Other:** enlarged kidneys, **neck circumference >40 cm (OSA)**, enlarged thyroid, BMI and waist circumference

---

## Step 3 — Baseline tests

**ESSENTIAL (everyone):**
- Blood: **sodium, potassium, creatinine with eGFR (2021 CKD-EPI), lipid profile, fasting glucose, uric acid, TSH**
- Urine dipstick: protein, blood, glucose
- **12-lead ECG:** LVH, ischaemia, arrhythmia
  - LVH: Sokolow–Lyon SV1+RV5 ≥35 mm; Cornell voltage SV3+RaVL >28 mm (men) / >20 mm (women); Cornell product >2440 mm·ms

**OPTIMAL (when organ damage, comorbidity or secondary hypertension suspected):**
- **Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR)** — first-morning spot
- **Echocardiography** — LVH, systolic/diastolic dysfunction, LA size, aortic root, coarctation (more sensitive than ECG)
- **Carotid ultrasound** — plaque, stenosis
- **Renal/adrenal imaging** — parenchymal disease, renal artery stenosis, adrenal mass
- **Brain CT/MRI** — if neurological symptoms or cognitive decline
- **Pulse wave velocity** — carotid-femoral >10 m/s (age 50–60) or brachial-ankle >18 m/s (age 60–70) = arterial stiffness
- **Ankle-brachial index** — <0.90 = peripheral artery disease
- **MMSE or clock drawing** — cognitive impairment
- **Fundoscopy** — if suspected hypertensive emergency, and in diabetes

## Step 4 — List risk factors, organ damage and established disease

| CV risk factors | HMOD | Established CV/kidney disease |
|---|---|---|
| Age ≥55 (men), ≥65 (women) | **Heart:** LVH (ECG or echo) | **Heart:** CAD, heart failure, atrial fibrillation |
| Male sex (men > women at age <65) | **Kidney:** eGFR <60 and/or UACR ≥30 mg/g | **Kidney:** CKD (eGFR <60 and/or UACR ≥30) |
| Family history of premature CVD | **Brain:** cognitive decline (MMSE) | **Brain:** stroke/TIA, vascular dementia |
| BMI ≥30 (lower in Asians) | **Arteries:** carotid plaque or coronary atherosclerosis (calcium score, angiography); increased arterial stiffness (PWV) | **Arteries:** peripheral artery disease (ABI <0.90) |
| Diabetes | | |
| Dyslipidaemia (high LDL, TG, low HDL) | | |

## Step 5 — Estimate total CV risk

**Simplified grid (example for a 60-year-old man; varies with age and sex):**

| | Elevated BP 130–139/80–89 | Grade 1 140–159/90–99 | Grade 2 ≥160/100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No risk factors, HMOD or CVD | Low | Low | Moderate–High |
| 1–2 risk factors | Low | Moderate | High |
| ≥3 risk factors or CKD G3a | Moderate | High | High |
| HMOD, CKD G3b–G5, diabetes or CVD | High | High | High |

**Better: use a validated, region-appropriate risk score** — SCORE2 (Europe), SCORE2-LAC (Latin America), PREVENT (USA), China-PAR, WHO risk charts (non-laboratory version available where cholesterol/glucose aren't measured). These may **underestimate risk in South Asian and African populations**.

**No risk score needed** if established CVD or HMOD — already high risk.

**OPTIMAL — refine apparently low–moderate risk with:** carotid plaque (ultrasound), Lp(a), coronary artery calcium score.

---

## Step 6 — Use the risk to time treatment

| Risk / BP | When to start drugs |
|---|---|
| **CVD, CKD, diabetes, HMOD, BP ≥160/100, or moderate–high risk** | **Immediately** after diagnosis is confirmed |
| **Low risk, BP 140–159/90–99** (ESSENTIAL) or **130–159/80–99** (OPTIMAL) | After **3 months of lifestyle change** if BP still above threshold |

Then go to `ish-htn-treatment-drug-strategy`.

---

## Guardrails

- **Risk is often underestimated in people who look low–moderate risk** — look for organ damage before labelling them low risk
- **Finding HMOD reclassifies the patient as high risk** — it changes management
- **Don't run a risk score in established CVD/HMOD** — they are high risk already
- **Standard scores underestimate risk in South Asians** — keep this in mind for Indian patients
- **Advanced vascular tests** (PWV, calcium score, carotid ultrasound) only when the result will change management
- **Always ask about BP-raising drugs** — NSAIDs and OCPs are common, reversible causes
