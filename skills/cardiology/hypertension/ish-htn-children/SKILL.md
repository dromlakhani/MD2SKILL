# Hypertension in Children & Adolescents (ISH 2026)

Global prevalence ~4% and rising, driven by childhood obesity. Childhood high BP tracks into adult CV and kidney disease.

---

## Step 1 — Measure correctly (ESSENTIAL)

- **sOBP at 2–3 visits** (adult protocol — see `ish-htn-diagnosis-bp-measurement`)
- **Calibrated manual auscultatory device**, or automated upper-arm device **validated specifically in children** (stridebp.org)
- **Cuff must fit the child's arm**

---

## Step 2 — Classify

| Age | Grade 1 hypertension | Grade 2 hypertension |
|---|---|---|
| **<16 years** | sOBP **≥95th percentile to <95th + 12 mmHg** for age, sex and height | sOBP **≥95th percentile + 12 mmHg** |
| **≥16 years** | **Adult thresholds** (≥140/90 grade 1; ≥160/100 grade 2) | |

Use population-based normative data.

---

## Step 3 — Confirm out of office (OPTIMAL)

- Out-of-office BP is usually needed to confirm the diagnosis
- **ABPM is preferred** for diagnosis in children (better studied)
- **Home BP** is better for long-term monitoring of treated children
- Normative tables exist for ABPM and home BP but vary by region, body size and obesity prevalence

---

## Step 4 — Decide whether to look for a secondary cause (ESSENTIAL)

Secondary causes are much more common in children than adults; primary hypertension dominates in adolescents. Investigate **selected** cases:

- **Age <6 years**
- **Age 6–12** without obesity and without family history of hypertension
- **Grade 2 hypertension without obesity**
- **Hypertension-mediated organ damage**
- **Severe acute hypertension**
- **Syndrome or family history** of secondary hypertension

---

## Step 5 — Treat

**Lifestyle (everyone):** especially **weight loss** in overweight/obese children.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| **Grade 1** | **Lifestyle alone for 3–6 months**; start drugs if hypertension persists |
| **Grade 2, hypertensive emergency, symptomatic hypertension, diabetes, CKD, organ damage** | **Start antihypertensives immediately** |

**Drug rules:** same principles as adults, but **prefer drugs studied in children**.

**Targets:**
- **<90th percentile** in children
- **<130/80 mmHg** in adolescents ≥16 years
- **CKD (OPTIMAL):** office and 24-h ambulatory BP **<75th percentile**; **<50th percentile** if proteinuria

---

## Step 6 — Refer (OPTIMAL)

Refer to a paediatric hypertension specialist if:
- You are uncomfortable prescribing antihypertensives in a child
- A secondary cause is suspected

---

## Guardrails

- **Use percentiles under 16** — adult cut-offs miss most childhood hypertension
- **Use child-validated devices and the right cuff** — oversized/undersized cuffs mislead
- **Young child (<6) with high BP = investigate** for a secondary cause
- **Give grade 1 a 3–6-month lifestyle trial** before drugs
- **Don't delay drugs** in grade 2, CKD, diabetes or organ damage
- **Tighter targets in CKD with proteinuria** (<50th percentile)
