# Hypertension Diagnosis & BP Measurement (ISH 2026)

The ISH 2026 guidelines give two levels of care: **ESSENTIAL** (minimum standard, low-resource settings) and **OPTIMAL** (high-resource). Give at least Essential care and add Optimal elements wherever possible.

---

## Step 1 — Make sure the reading is a standardised office BP (sOBP)

Casual office readings are not valid for diagnosis — they usually overestimate BP. Check the reading was taken this way (ESSENTIAL):

- **Before:** no smoking, caffeine or exercise for 30 min; empty bladder; quiet room, comfortable temperature; seated and resting 3–5 min; nobody talks
- **Position:** back supported, feet flat, legs uncrossed, arm on table with mid-arm at heart level, arm bare
- **Device:** validated automated upper-arm cuff (lists at stridebp.org), or calibrated manual auscultatory device (Korotkoff 1 = SBP, 5 = DBP)
- **Cuff:** sized to mid-arm circumference (small cuff overestimates, large cuff underestimates)
- **Protocol:** 3 readings, 30–60 s (about 1 min) apart → **average the last 2**

OPTIMAL extras at first visit:
- Measure **both arms**; use the higher arm. A consistent SBP difference >10 mmHg → use the higher arm; >20 mmHg → investigate
- **Standing BP** if symptoms of orthostasis, age >65, diabetes or neurodegenerative disease. Orthostatic hypotension = SBP fall ≥20 and/or DBP fall ≥10 mmHg within 3 min of standing

---

## Step 2 — Classify the office BP

Use the average of at least 2 visits (last 2 of 3 readings each visit).

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | | Diastolic (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | <130 | and | <80 |
| **Elevated BP** | 130–139 | and/or | 80–89 |
| **Grade 1 hypertension** | 140–159 | and/or | 90–99 |
| **Grade 2 hypertension** | ≥160 | and/or | ≥100 |

"Elevated BP" is not normal — it needs clinical attention and usually treatment (see `ish-htn-treatment-drug-strategy`).

---

## Step 3 — Decide how to confirm the diagnosis

| sOBP at first visit | ESSENTIAL | OPTIMAL |
|---|---|---|
| **<130/80** | Re-measure annually | Re-measure annually |
| **130–159 / 80–99** | Confirm at **2–3 visits, 1–4 weeks apart** | Confirm with **out-of-office BP** (home or ambulatory) — high chance of white-coat or masked hypertension |
| **≥160/100** | Confirm **within days to weeks** | Same |

**Single-visit diagnosis only if** sOBP ≥180/110 mmHg **and** there is hypertension-mediated organ damage (HMOD).

**Hypertension is diagnosed** when sOBP is ≥140/90 mmHg on at least 2 visits.

---

## Step 4 — Out-of-office thresholds

| Method | Protocol | Elevated BP | Hypertension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standardised office** | ≥2 visits, 3 readings each, average last 2 | 130–139/80–89 | **≥140/90** |
| **Standardised home (sHBPM)** | 7 days (≥3 days, ≥12 readings), morning and evening, 2 readings each 1 min apart after 5 min rest; discard day 1 | 130–134/80–84 | **≥135/85** |
| **24-h ambulatory (ABPM)** — 24-h average | ≥20 awake + ≥7 asleep readings, every 20–30 min | 125–129/75–79 | **≥130/80** |
| ABPM — daytime (awake) | | 130–134/80–84 | **≥135/85** |
| ABPM — night-time (asleep) | | 110–119/65–69 | **≥120/70** |

- **sHBPM is the preferred main method** for diagnosis and long-term monitoring (OPTIMAL)
- **ABPM** is preferred when home BP is not possible, office and home disagree, in children and pregnancy, suspected nocturnal hypertension, and before renal denervation. One 24-h recording may not suffice — repeat to confirm
- **Pharmacy BP:** threshold about 135/85; useful for screening and follow-up
- **Kiosk BP:** screening only, not diagnosis
- **Cuffless devices and AI smartwatches:** **not** for diagnosis or management — confirm with cuff BP
- **Wrist-cuff devices:** only validated ones, with arm supported and wrist at heart level

---

## Step 5 — Identify the hypertension phenotype

| Phenotype | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **White-coat hypertension** | Office hypertension, normal home/ambulatory BP (10–30% of clinic patients) | Confirm with repeat office + out-of-office BP. **No drugs** if out-of-office BP clearly normal. Lifestyle + periodic home/ABPM (~25% develop sustained hypertension in 5 years). Consider drugs only if HMOD present, diagnosis confirmed, out-of-office BP near threshold, and other causes of organ damage excluded |
| **Masked hypertension** | Normal/elevated office BP, hypertension at home/ambulatory (10–30%) | Confirm with repeat office and out-of-office BP. **Treat** — aim to normalise out-of-office BP. CV risk close to sustained hypertension |
| **Nocturnal hypertension** | Night-time (asleep) average ≥120/70 on ABPM or home night-time device | One 24-h recording is not enough — repeat. Look for poor sleep and **OSA**; in elderly (≥80) or diabetes, consider autonomic dysfunction. Treat with long-acting drugs to control 24-h BP + salt restriction + sleep disorder management. **No evidence** for bedtime dosing or for treating isolated nocturnal hypertension |
| **High BP variability** | Common at age ≥70 | No agreed definition. Average BP remains the target; long-acting DHP-CCBs reduce variability most |

**Isolated systolic hypertension** is the commonest form after age 60 (also common in children and young adults). Isolated diastolic hypertension is less common and seen in younger adults.

---

## Guardrails

- **Never diagnose on a casual office reading** — use the full sOBP protocol
- **Never diagnose at a single visit** unless ≥180/110 with organ damage
- **Use the right threshold for the method** — home 135/85 and 24-h ABPM 130/80 are lower than office 140/90
- **Don't treat white-coat hypertension** when out-of-office BP is clearly normal
- **Don't miss masked hypertension** — it carries near-sustained-hypertension risk and needs treatment
- **Cuffless and smartwatch BP do not count** for diagnosis
- **Wrong cuff size is the commonest measurement error** — check it every time
