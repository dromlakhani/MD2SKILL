# Hypertension Treatment & Drug Strategy (ISH 2026)

**Aim: BP controlled to target within 3 months.** Review monthly until controlled.

---

## Step 1 — Thresholds and targets

| | ESSENTIAL | OPTIMAL |
|---|---|---|
| **BP used** | Standardised office BP | Standardised office BP, confirmed with home or ambulatory BP |
| **Treatment threshold** | **≥140/90** | **≥130/80** (focus on systolic if DBP 80–89) |
| **Target — office** | **<140/90** | **<130/80** |
| **Target — home** | — | **<130/80** |
| **Target — ABPM** | — | 24-h **<125/75**; daytime **<130/80**; night-time **<110/65** |

**Individualise threshold and target** in age >85, frailty, symptomatic orthostatic hypotension, or limited life expectancy. Treating at ≥130/80 and targeting <130/80 is **not** appropriate for everyone.

Every 10 mmHg SBP fall ≈ 20% fewer major CV events, 30–40% less stroke and heart failure, 17% less CHD.

---

## Step 2 — When to start drugs

| Situation | Start |
|---|---|
| **CVD, CKD, diabetes, HMOD, BP ≥160/100, or moderate–high CV risk** | **Immediately** after confirming diagnosis |
| **Low CV risk, BP 140–159/90–99** (ESSENTIAL) or **130–159/80–99** (OPTIMAL) | After **3 months of lifestyle change** if still above threshold |

Lifestyle change applies **before and during** drug treatment in everyone.

---

## Step 3 — Lifestyle prescription (everyone)

Combined changes can lower SBP by >10 mmHg — comparable to 1–2 drugs.

- **Salt:** ≤5 g salt (2 g sodium) per day — about 1 teaspoon; avoid soy sauce, fast food, processed foods, breads, salty cereals
- **Potassium:** more fruit, vegetables, legumes, low-fat dairy; potassium-enriched low-sodium salt (**caution in kidney disease**)
- **Diet:** DASH-style; leafy vegetables, beans, beetroot, nuts, seeds; limit added sugar; high fibre
- **Drinks:** beetroot juice, green/black tea, hibiscus tea, pomegranate juice, unsweetened cocoa may help
- **Alcohol:** avoid or limit to **2 standard drinks per week**; no binge drinking
- **Weight:** waist-to-height ratio <0.5; ethnic-specific BMI and waist cut-offs
- **Exercise:** aerobic ≥30 min on 5–7 days/week; resistance/HIIT 2–3 days/week
- **Stress:** mindfulness, yoga, meditation, music ≥30 min 3×/week
- **Sleep:** 7–8 h; no alcohol or large meals within 3 h of sleep; treat sleep apnoea
- **Smoking/vaping:** stop completely
- **Avoid alternative/traditional medicines** for hypertension

---

## Step 4 — Choose the starting regimen

| sOBP | Start with |
|---|---|
| **≥140/90 (hypertension)** | **Two-drug single-pill combination** (ESSENTIAL and OPTIMAL) |
| **130–139/80–89 (elevated BP)** — OPTIMAL | **Monotherapy** (preferred), or low-dose 2-drug single pill if high risk with SBP close to 140 |
| **Age >85, frailty, symptomatic orthostatic hypotension, limited life expectancy** | Consider starting with monotherapy |

**ESSENTIAL:** use whatever drugs are available that best match the ideal characteristics — proven outcome benefit, once-daily 24-h control, affordable, well tolerated, evidence in the population treated.

Expected SBP fall: monotherapy ~9 mmHg; two drugs ~15 mmHg; three drugs ~20 mmHg. **Prefer adding drugs at low–standard doses over up-titrating one drug** (dose escalation gives little extra BP fall but more side effects).

---

## Step 5 — The ISH core drug ladder (OPTIMAL)

**A** = ACEi or ARB · **C** = dihydropyridine CCB · **D** = thiazide or thiazide-like diuretic

| Step | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **1** | **A + C** low-dose (half max dose) single pill. Consider **A + D** if oedema, CCB intolerance or early heart failure |
| **2** | **A + C** full dose, **or** low-dose **A + C + D** |
| **3** | **A + C + D** full dose (triple) |
| **4 — resistant** | Add **spironolactone** (preferred). Others: amiloride, eplerenone, beta-blocker, clonidine, doxazosin, sacubitril/valsartan. New: baxdrostat, lorundrostat, aprocitentan, esaxerenone (availability varies) |
| **5** | Add any class not yet used; refer to expert centre / consider renal denervation — see `ish-htn-resistant-and-secondary` |

**Review monthly; intensify whenever BP is above target.**

**Rules at every step:**
- **Give all drugs together in the morning** (or at the most convenient time) — no evidence for bedtime dosing
- **Single-pill combinations** (2, 3 or 4 drugs) whenever available; free combinations if unaffordable
- **Do not combine ACEi + ARB + renin inhibitor**
- **Caution with MRA or K-sparing drugs** if eGFR <30 or K⁺ >4.5 mmol/L
- **Diltiazem or verapamil** if DHP-CCB unavailable or not tolerated
- **Beta-blockers at any step** if: heart failure, angina, post-MI, AF needing rate control, SVT/some ventricular arrhythmias, pregnancy or planning pregnancy

---

## Step 6 — Adjust for comorbidities

| Comorbidity | Drug choice |
|---|---|
| **Type 2 diabetes** | ACEi/ARB preferred (especially UACR ≥30 mg/g). Add **SGLT2i** (esp. CKD/HF) and/or **GLP-1 RA** (esp. obesity/high CV risk). Use home/ambulatory BP (masked, nocturnal and resistant hypertension common) |
| **CKD** | Include RAS blocker (esp. albuminuria); diuretics usually needed — **loop diuretic or chlorthalidone if eGFR <30**. **SGLT2i if eGFR >20**. Finerenone in diabetes with normal K⁺ and UACR ≥30 (consider without diabetes); GLP-1 RA may be added. **Check eGFR and K⁺ 2–4 weeks** after starting/titrating RAS blocker, SGLT2i, MRA or diuretic; eGFR fall >30% → consider stopping (usually reversible, often over-diuresis) |
| **Coronary artery disease** | RAS blocker, beta-blocker, antiplatelet, intensive lipid lowering, lifestyle |
| **Previous stroke/TIA** | RAS blocker + diuretic first; CCB also fine. Beta-blockers less effective for stroke prevention |
| **Heart failure** | RAS blocker, beta-blocker, ARNI, MRA, SGLT2i per HF guidelines; add DHP-CCB if BP uncontrolled |
| **Atrial fibrillation** | Manage as general population; beta-blocker for rate control (<80/min). ≥3 BP readings each time. **SBP <160 before starting anticoagulation, keep <140** |
| **COPD** | As general population, beta-blockers when indicated |
| **Obstructive sleep apnoea** | Weight loss; MRA useful in resistant hypertension. CPAP gives only modest BP fall |
| **Dyslipidaemia** | Most hypertensives >50 years benefit from lipid lowering based on CV risk |
| **Obesity** | Weight loss; GLP-1 RA and dual GIP/GLP-1 RA lower BP and improve CV outcomes |
| **HIV** | Manage as general population (nocturnal hypertension common) |

**Ethnicity:** Black/African-descent — **CCB + diuretic** first line (single pill); ARB preferred over ACEi (angioedema, cough). Asian — standard treatment; lower BMI/waist cut-offs.

**Seasonal variation:** BP ~5 mmHg lower in hot weather. Consider down-titration if BP well below target with symptoms; extreme heat with dehydration risks hypotension on diuretics/RAS blockers.

---

## Step 7 — Adherence (ESSENTIAL)

Non-adherence affects 30–50% and is a leading cause of poor control.

- Check adherence at every visit, especially before adding drugs
- Reduce pill burden — single-pill combinations, once-daily dosing
- Link pill-taking to daily habits; dosette boxes, reminders
- OPTIMAL: self-management counselling, long-term home BP, apps/SMS, telemedicine, team-based care (nurses, pharmacists, community health workers)
- Objective check if needed: urine/plasma drug detection

---

## Step 8 — Follow-up

- **Monthly** until target reached (in person or virtual)
- **Well-controlled low-risk:** every 6–12 months
- **Well-controlled high-risk:** every 3–6 months
- **Long-term home BP monitoring** (1–2 duplicate readings per week or month; not daily)
- **Never accept BP above target without action**

---

## Guardrails

- **Don't start with monotherapy for BP ≥140/90** unless elderly/frail — single-pill dual therapy is the default
- **Don't up-titrate one drug** when adding a second at low dose would work better
- **Don't combine ACEi with ARB** (or a renin inhibitor)
- **Don't give spironolactone if K⁺ >4.5 or eGFR <30** without close monitoring
- **Don't use bedtime dosing** for its own sake — no outcome benefit
- **Confirm with home/ambulatory BP** when treating elevated BP or targeting <130/80
- **Individualise in >85, frail, orthostatic or limited life expectancy**
- **Check adherence before calling it treatment failure**
