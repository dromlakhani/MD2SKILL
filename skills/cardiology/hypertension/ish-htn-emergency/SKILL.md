# Hypertensive Emergency (ISH 2026)

**Hypertensive emergency = very high BP (no fixed threshold, usually ≥180/120 mmHg) WITH acute organ damage** (retina, brain, heart, large arteries, kidneys). Needs rapid assessment and immediate, usually IV, BP lowering.

---

## Step 1 — Is there acute organ damage?

**No acute organ damage** (asymptomatic markedly raised BP) → **not an emergency.** Usually no admission or IV therapy. **Oral antihypertensives + close follow-up.**

**Acute organ damage present** → emergency. Identify the presentation:

| Presentation | Features |
|---|---|
| **Malignant hypertension** | Very high BP + acute microvascular damage: bilateral retinal haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema |
| **Hypertensive encephalopathy** | Very high BP + lethargy, seizures, cortical blindness or coma, no other cause |
| **Hypertensive thrombotic microangiopathy** | Very high BP + haemolysis and thrombocytopenia, no other cause, improves with BP lowering |
| **Other** | Very high BP with stroke, acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary oedema, aortic dissection, severe preeclampsia/eclampsia |

---

## Step 2 — Investigate

**ESSENTIAL:**
- Cardiovascular and neurological examination
- Haemoglobin, platelets, creatinine, sodium, potassium, **LDH**, urinalysis (protein, sediment)
- **Fundoscopy, ECG**

**OPTIMAL (by presentation):** troponin, chest X-ray, echocardiogram, brain CT/MRI, CT angiography of thorax and abdomen.

A **secondary cause is found in 20–40%** of malignant hypertension — work it up.

---

## Step 3 — Lower BP by presentation (ESSENTIAL)

Goal: lower BP to safer levels to stop further organ damage **while avoiding hypotension**. Speed and target depend on the organ involved.

| Presentation | Timeline and target | First-line | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malignant hypertension** | Several hours; **MAP ↓ 20–25%** | Labetalol, nicardipine | Nitroprusside, urapidil |
| **Hypertensive encephalopathy** | Immediate; **MAP ↓ 20–25%** | Labetalol, nicardipine | Nitroprusside |
| **Acute ischaemic stroke, thrombolysis planned**, BP >185/110 | 1 h; **MAP ↓ ~15%** | Labetalol, nicardipine | Nitroprusside |
| **Acute ischaemic stroke, BP >220/120** | 1 h; **MAP ↓ ~15%** | Labetalol, nicardipine | Nitroprusside |
| **Acute haemorrhagic stroke, SBP >180** | Immediate; **SBP <180 and >130** | Labetalol, nicardipine | Urapidil |
| **Acute coronary event** | Immediate; **SBP <140** | Nitroglycerine, labetalol | Urapidil |
| **Acute pulmonary oedema** | Immediate; **SBP <140** | Nitroprusside or nitroglycerine + loop diuretic | Urapidil + loop diuretic |
| **Acute aortic dissection** | Immediate; **SBP <120 and HR <60** | Esmolol + nitroprusside or nitroglycerine or nicardipine | Labetalol or metoprolol |
| **Severe preeclampsia, eclampsia, HELLP** | Immediate; **SBP <160 and DBP <105** | Labetalol or nicardipine + **magnesium sulphate** | — |

**Key points:**
- **Rapid lowering** is needed in **pulmonary oedema and aortic dissection**
- **BP up to 220/120 may be tolerated temporarily** in acute ischaemic stroke **when reperfusion is not planned**
- **Labetalol and nicardipine** are safe in most emergencies; **nitroglycerine and nitroprusside** for cardiac and aortic emergencies. Have them available wherever emergencies are managed

---

## Step 4 — Follow-up (ESSENTIAL)

Patients after a hypertensive emergency have high CV and renal risk.

- **Investigate the underlying cause and organ damage** (secondary causes common)
- **Review monthly** until target BP achieved
- **Long-term follow-up**

---

## Guardrails

- **Very high BP alone is not an emergency** — organ damage defines it; asymptomatic severe hypertension is managed orally
- **Don't drop BP too fast or too far** (except aortic dissection and pulmonary oedema) — target a 20–25% MAP fall over hours in malignant hypertension/encephalopathy
- **Ischaemic stroke without reperfusion tolerates up to 220/120** — don't over-treat
- **Aortic dissection needs both SBP <120 and HR <60** — beta-blockade first
- **Magnesium sulphate with antihypertensive** in severe preeclampsia/eclampsia
- **Look for a secondary cause** — present in 20–40% of malignant hypertension
- **Drug choice depends on local availability and expertise**
