# Resistant & Secondary Hypertension (ISH 2026)

Uncontrolled BP is most often due to **clinical inertia or non-adherence**. Rule those out before labelling the patient resistant.

---

## Step 1 — Define apparent resistant hypertension

**sOBP ≥140/90 mmHg on ≥3 antihypertensive drugs at optimal or maximally tolerated doses, including a diuretic.**

About **50%** of people labelled resistant have **pseudo-resistance**. True resistant hypertension affects 5–10% of hypertensives and carries high CV risk.

---

## Step 2 — Exclude pseudo-resistance (ESSENTIAL)

Check each:

- **Non-standardised office BP** — repeat with full sOBP protocol (`ish-htn-diagnosis-bp-measurement`)
- **White-coat effect** — confirm with home or ambulatory BP (OPTIMAL: use sHBPM/ABPM to diagnose true resistance)
- **Non-adherence** — ask, pill counts, refill data; drug detection in urine/plasma if available
- **Suboptimal regimen** — wrong doses, no diuretic, wrong diuretic for eGFR
- **Substance/drug-induced** — NSAIDs, high-dose paracetamol, combined OCP, SNRIs/MAOIs/tricyclics, VEGF/TKIs, ciclosporin, decongestants, stimulants, ADHD drugs, glucocorticoids, EPO/HIF inhibitors, triptans, excess caffeine/energy drinks, liquorice, ephedra, St John's wort, yohimbine, ginseng
- **Secondary hypertension** (Step 5)

Reduce or remove BP-raising substances where possible. If they are needed, treat BP to target anyway.

---

## Step 3 — Optimise treatment (ESSENTIAL)

1. Lifestyle (especially salt) + review adherence
2. **Diuretic-based regimen at maximally tolerated doses** — **loop diuretic or chlorthalidone if eGFR <30**
3. **Add low-dose spironolactone as the 4th drug** if **K⁺ <4.5 mmol/L and eGFR ≥30**, with close K⁺/eGFR monitoring
4. Add any available class not already in use
5. Simplify — single pills, once daily

**OPTIMAL — if spironolactone not tolerated or BP still high:**
- Amiloride, eplerenone, beta-blocker, clonidine, doxazosin, sacubitril/valsartan
- **New classes** (approval and availability vary by country):
  - **Aldosterone synthase inhibitors** — baxdrostat (approved for uncontrolled/resistant), lorundrostat (under review). Watch **hyperkalaemia (worse in CKD)** and **hyponatraemia** — monitor K⁺ and Na⁺
  - **Endothelin receptor antagonist** — aprocitentan (approved in some regions). Watch **fluid retention, oedema, weight gain, heart failure** — more in CKD and pre-existing CVD
  - **Non-steroidal MRA** — esaxerenone (Japan). K⁺ and renal monitoring as other RAAS inhibitors
  - **Angiotensinogen siRNA** — zilebesiran (not yet marketed; 6-monthly SC). Effect can't be switched off — hypotension, renal change, hyperkalaemia risks
- Refer to an expert centre for further investigation and titration

---

## Step 4 — Renal denervation (OPTIMAL, expert centres only)

BP fall similar to one extra drug; not all patients respond.

**Indications:**
- True resistant hypertension uncontrolled on ≥4 drugs at max tolerated doses (ACEi/ARB + CCB + thiazide/thiazide-like + aldosterone-targeting drug), **or**
- Uncontrolled on fewer drugs because of intolerable side effects

**Requirements:**
- Non-adherence, white-coat effect (24-h ABPM) and secondary hypertension excluded
- **eGFR ≥40**
- No renal artery abnormalities
- CV risk, life expectancy and preferences considered
- Expert hypertension team; informed shared decision

---

## Step 5 — When to suspect secondary hypertension (ESSENTIAL)

A cause is found in ≥10% of hypertensives (more in the young). Consider screening if:

- **Age <25**, especially very high BP, organ damage, no obesity, no family history
- **Spontaneous or diuretic-induced hypokalaemia**
- **Obesity, snoring, daytime sleepiness** (OSA)
- **True resistant hypertension**
- **Malignant hypertension** or very high BP (e.g. >180/120)
- **Sudden onset or sudden worsening** of BP control
- Strong clinical or laboratory clues

These prompt **consideration**, not mandatory investigation every time. **Exclude pseudo-resistance and drug-induced hypertension first.** Basic screen = history, examination, basic labs.

---

## Step 6 — The common secondary causes

| Cause | Prevalence | Clues | First test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary aldosteronism** | 5–10% (up to 20% in resistant) | Hypokalaemia (but >60% normokalaemic) | **Aldosterone-to-renin ratio** — stop MRAs 4 weeks before; ensure normal K⁺; no sodium restriction |
| **Renal parenchymal disease** | 5–10% | Often silent | **Urine dipstick + eGFR** |
| **Obstructive sleep apnoea** | ~20% (>30% in resistant) | Obesity, resistant or nocturnal hypertension | **STOP-Bang** or other validated tool; sleep study |
| **Renal artery stenosis** | 2–5% | Atherosclerosis; women <30 (fibromuscular dysplasia); abdominal bruit; falling eGFR | Renal artery imaging |
| **Substance/drug-induced** | 2–4% | Drug history | Stop/replace drug |

**Uncommon:** Cushing's, thyroid disease, **coarctation** (children/young; arm BP > leg BP, radio-femoral delay), **phaeochromocytoma** (very rare, variable presentation).

**OPTIMAL:** choose further tests based on history, exam and basic results; refer to a specialist centre for full secondary workup — it is complex and costly.

---

## Guardrails

- **Half of "resistant" hypertension is pseudo-resistance** — always exclude it first
- **Confirm resistance with home or ambulatory BP** before escalating
- **Spironolactone is the preferred 4th drug** — not a beta-blocker or alpha-blocker
- **Hypokalaemia is absent in most primary aldosteronism** — normal K⁺ doesn't exclude it
- **Stop MRAs 4 weeks before an aldosterone-renin ratio** and correct K⁺
- **Watch K⁺ and Na⁺ with aldosterone synthase inhibitors**, fluid with aprocitentan
- **Renal denervation is not first-line** — only after optimised drugs, in expert centres, eGFR ≥40
- **New drug availability varies** — check local approval before recommending
